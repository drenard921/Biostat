17.1 When you are investigating the relationship between two continuous random variables, why is it important to create a scatter plot of the data?

Creating a scatter plot is essential when examining the relationship between two continuous random variables because it provides a clear visual representation of their association. It allows us to assess whether the relationship is linear, detect outliers, and spot any unusual patterns such as clusters or non-constant variance. These visual cues help determine whether a correlation or linear regression is appropriate. Without a scatter plot, we risk overlooking important features that could affect the validity of our analysis.

18.4 Why is it dangerous to extrapolate an estimated linear regression line outside the range of the observed data values?

Extrapolating a linear regression line outside the range of observed data is risky because the model is only valid within the scope of the data used to fit it. The relationship between the predictor and outcome variables may not remain linear beyond the observed range, and the assumptions of the regression model—such as linearity, constant variance, and independence—may no longer hold. Predictions made in these unobserved regions can be highly inaccurate or misleading. Without empirical data to support those regions, there’s no way to verify whether the trend continues or changes direction.
